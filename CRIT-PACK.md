# Critique pack — Advertiser Experience Strategy

For running a real critique with people who were not in the build. Everything the
facilitator needs is here; the deck itself is `Advertiser Experience Strategy.dc.html`.

**Why this exists.** Every critique of this deck so far has been a self-critique by its
author. That catches execution drift — and has, repeatedly — but structurally cannot
catch what the author failed to consider. The second self-pass yielded exactly one
finding. That is the signal to bring other eyes in.

Suggested: **45 minutes, 3–5 people, at least two from outside Advertiser Experience.**

---

## 1 · Context to share first (5 min)

**Audience.** Walmart Ads leadership, who will confirm a charter, name partner owners,
and protect capacity. Then circulated to product and channel teams as the reference
record.

**The ask.** Four decisions — confirm the charter, commit the partners, protect the
capacity, require the evidence.

**Stage.** Refinement, not exploration. The narrative is settled; craft has been through
several passes. This crit is for the argument, not the pixels.

**Constraints.** 31 core slides plus a 33-slide appendix, deliberately sized. Fixed
1280×720. Walmart Design System. Every quantitative claim must carry source, population
and denominator.

---

## 2 · What feedback is wanted — and what is not

**Wanted.**
- Does the argument hold for someone who was not in the build?
- Where would a leader push back, and is the deck ready for it?
- What is asserted that is not evidenced?
- What would a channel or domain lead object to?

**Not up for debate in this session.** These were settled in the revision brief;
reopening them turns a crit into a rewrite:
- The four priorities and their framing
- The retirement of the foundation-first sequencing ask
- The 31-slide core and the core/appendix split
- Title case in the core, sentence case in the appendix

If someone wants to reopen one, capture it as a separate decision — do not resolve it here.

## 4 · The six slides that will draw fire (20 min)

Go to these directly. Everything else can be read async.

| Slide | Why it draws fire | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **33 · Capacity** | The ask has no magnitude. Two lines read "size to confirm" and "owner to name". | Does the room accept an unsized ask, or does it stop here? It should stop here. |
| **03 · The decision** | Four asks presented together. No alternatives were considered. | Are these severable? What if leadership approves two of four? |
| **22 · Display reset** | Puts Goal-Based Buying and the template-first assumption under "reassess". | Whoever owns that programme will hear cancellation. Does the Continue column land first? |
| **29 · Dispositions** | Eight areas given a disposition. These are the author's reading, not agreements. | Does anyone mistake this for a decision already taken? If so, the slide needs relabelling. |
| **12 · Diagnosis** | Generalises from evidence that is entirely in-product and self-serve-weighted. | The scope caveat is now on the slide. Is it enough, or does it need saying aloud? |
| **28 · Roadmap** | Stage tags and windows imply more readiness than exists. | Does "quarter placement is not readiness" actually survive contact? |

## 5 · Feedback rules

Use the frame, out loud:

- **"I notice…"** — observation, not judgement
- **"I wonder…"** — a question, genuinely open
- **"What if…"** — an alternative, offered not insisted
- **"I think… because…"** — an opinion, with its reason attached

Two house rules for this deck specifically:

1. **Tie feedback to the audience or the evidence**, not to taste. "I'd use a different
   chart" is not actionable; "a leader won't believe this without a denominator" is.
2. **Separate "this is wrong" from "this is missing."** The deck has known holes that are
   deliberate — unfilled baselines, unsized capacity, unnamed partners. Confirming a hole
   is not a finding.
