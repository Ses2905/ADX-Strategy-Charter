# Jobs-to-Be-Done — Walmart advertisers

Working analysis, not deck content. Every claim below traces to a slide or a verbatim
already in the deck. Anything inferred is labelled **hypothesis** — no new research was
conducted and no quotes, metrics or stakeholder views were invented.

**Why this exists:** the deck's evidence is almost entirely *functional* — task success,
drop-off, dead clicks, findings counts. JTBD's other two dimensions are present in the
raw VOC but never named, and one of them is unaddressed and unmeasured.

---

## The core job

> **When** I have a budget and a commercial target at Walmart,
> **I want to** turn that budget into a result I can measure,
> **so I can** justify the investment and repeat it.

Note what this is *not*: nobody hires Walmart Ads to "manage campaigns." Campaign
management is a cost of the job, which is why friction there reads as tax rather than
as a missing feature.

---

## The three dimensions

### Functional — thoroughly evidenced

Five sub-jobs, taken directly from slide 11's struggle → need table:

| Sub-job | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Decide what to buy before committing | "Building campaigns before understanding likely outcomes" |
| Launch without relearning per product | Slide 12: eight stages, six platform boundaries |
| Keep spend under control | "Budget and pacing uncertainty" |
| Know what happened and why | "Inconsistent and difficult reporting" |
| Know what to change next | "Limited transparency into how optimization decides" |

The deck answers these well. Priorities 01–04 map to them almost one to one.

### Emotional — present in the evidence, never named

The verbatims are not feature requests. They are statements of powerlessness:

- *"Without any information, how can I optimize my campaign?"* — not a missing report;
  an inability to act.
- *"Platform management is a mess. I have to choose from six dropdowns to get anything
  done."* — effort experienced as disrespect for their time.
- *"Reports are not accurate. Doesn't match the actual data in the summary table."* —
  a trust failure, not a data-quality ticket.

The deck already holds the two strongest emotional measures and files both as friction:

- **13,652 rage clicks** (slide 10). That is an emotion metric presented as a behavioural one.
- **r = 0.89** between experience rating and perceived effectiveness (slide 09). The
  deck correctly refuses the causal claim, but the finding *is* the emotional job:
  advertisers do not hold "hard to use" and "doesn't work" apart.

**Implication.** Slide 32's Level 04 — "Do trust and control improve?" — is the
emotional dimension, but its measures (budget comprehension, reporting comprehension,
error recovery) are functional proxies. That is defensible; it should be named as
trust rather than left implicit.

### Social — largely absent from the deck

Every advertiser in the segment map answers to someone.

| Who | Judged on | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Media buyer / trader | Explaining performance to a client or lead | Appendix: agency investment map |
| Agency commerce lead | Running Walmart efficiently across many clients | "Automation, interoperability and clean data are what earn agency allocation" |
| Brand analytics | Defending incrementality; holds veto on scale | Appendix: brand investment map |
| 3P seller | Their own P&L — failure is personal money | "Seller P&L" as investment source |

**The sharpest gap.** The deck treats reporting as *comprehension*. The evidence says
it is also *defensibility*. "Reports are not accurate — doesn't match the summary table"
is not merely confusing; it is unusable as evidence in front of a client or a CFO. An
advertiser who cannot explain the number will not defend the spend, whatever the number
says.

**Hypothesis, not finding:** social risk is why reporting inconsistency outranks its raw
severity. Testable — ask advertisers who they report to and what happens when the
numbers disagree. Not currently in the research corpus.

## Job stages against the deck's lifecycle

The deck's target lifecycle (slide 14) is Discover → Plan → Activate → Manage →
Measure → Optimize. Mapped to the canonical stages:

| JTBD stage | Deck stage | State |
|---|---|---|
| Define | Discover | Covered |
| Locate | Discover | Covered |
| Prepare | Plan | Covered — and correctly elevated out of campaign creation |
| **Confirm** | — | **No stage.** "Am I about to do the right thing?" |
| Execute | Activate | Covered |
| Monitor | Manage | Covered |
| Modify | Optimize | Covered |
| Conclude | — | **No stage** in the core lifecycle |

**Confirm** is the sharper of the two gaps. The deck's own answer already exists —
slide 17 targets "validate before launch, not after spend" — but it sits as a feature
of Simplify rather than as a stage of the job. Naming it would connect the 46.2%
drop-off (people abandoning *before* a campaign exists) to a job stage rather than to
a flow problem.

**Conclude** exists for sellers (slide 18: Return) but not in the general lifecycle.
The renewal decision — *do I do this again next quarter* — is where budget is actually
won or lost, and no stage owns it.

## Where current solutions underserve

Ranked by gap between job importance and current support, using the deck's evidence.

1. **Confirm before committing.** High importance, no stage, 46.2% drop-off before a
   campaign exists. Priority 01 addresses the mechanics; nothing owns the job.
2. **Defend the result to someone else.** High importance, unaddressed, unmeasured.
   No slide asks whether an advertiser can explain what happened.
3. **Conclude and re-plan.** High importance at renewal, covered only for sellers.
4. **Know what to change next.** High importance, addressed by Priority 04 — correctly
   gated on signal reliability and advertiser control.
5. **Launch without relearning.** Well covered by Priorities 01 and 03.

---

## Design implications

**Recommendations, not changes.** The deck's measures are the team's commitments;
adding one is a decision, not an edit.

1. **Name Confirm as a lifecycle stage** rather than leaving pre-launch validation as a
   Simplify feature. It reframes the funnel evidence from "the flow is bad" to "a stage
   of the job has no home."
2. **Add one social measure to slide 32's Level 04** — whether an advertiser can explain
   the result to the person who funds it. Task-level, testable, and it is the only
   dimension of the core job currently unmeasured.
3. **Rename Level 04 to name trust explicitly.** Its measures are already the right ones.
4. **Add a Conclude stage** or state deliberately that renewal sits outside the lifecycle.
5. **Put "who do you report to, and what happens when the numbers disagree?" into the
   next research round.** It is the one question that would convert the social dimension
   from hypothesis to evidence.

None of these change the strategy. They change what the strategy is measured on, which
is why they belong to you rather than to an edit.
