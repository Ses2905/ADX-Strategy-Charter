# Leadership Q&A — Advertiser Experience Strategy

Prep for the room. Ranked by likelihood × damage, not by deck order.

Three questions the deck **cannot currently answer**. They are marked ⛔ and each
names the input that closes it. Everything else has an answer grounded in a slide.

---

## ⛔ 1. "How much capacity are you actually asking for?"

**Why it lands.** Ask 03 is "protect the capacity" and slide 33 shows the gap
qualitatively — no dedicated front-end engineering, no service owner, evidence
capability unowned — but never states a magnitude. This is the first question and
the deck has no slide that answers it.

**What closes it.** A number for: dedicated front-end engineering on Priority 03, a
named service owner, and the analytics/research-ops commitment behind the evidence
capability. Bring a range with what each tier buys, not a single figure.

**If asked before you have it.** "I'm not going to invent that number in this room.
What I can tell you is what is unowned today and what it blocks — that's slide 33 —
and I'll come back with a sized ask against those three lines."

---

## ⛔ 2. "What is this worth?"

**Why it lands.** The deck sizes the *problem* thoroughly — NPS −27 across 1,350
responses, 46.2% drop-off in Display ad group setup, 125 audit findings — and never
sizes the *prize*. The market slide gives $142B by 2030, but that's the market, not
Walmart's share and not what an experience improvement returns.

**What closes it.** Task-level baselines that don't exist yet. The two that do exist
(NPS, experience rating) are historical and predominantly Sponsored Search, so they
can't carry a value estimate.

**If asked.** "We haven't sized it, and I'd rather say that than show you a number I
can't defend. The instrumentation that would let us size it is part of the ask —
it's the first thing the evidence capability produces."

**Do not** improvise a revenue or payback figure. The deck's credibility rests on
every number having a source, a population and a denominator.

---

## ⛔ 3. "Whose roadmap shrinks to pay for this?"

**Why it lands.** Protected capacity comes from somewhere, and the deck never says
whose. Channel teams bear the near-term cost and they will be in the room. This is
the deck's biggest unaddressed political risk.

**What closes it.** A named trade-off — which committed items move, and who agreed.
Slide 29's dispositions are proposals, not agreements; do not present them as settled.

**If asked.** "Slide 29 is my read, not a decision. What I'm asking for here is the
standard and the owner; which specific items reshape is the portfolio conversation,
and I'd want the channel leads in it."

---

## 4. "What did you consider and reject?"

**Answer.** The alternative is the current default: channel-led delivery, reconciled
afterwards. Slide 03's "what continues without it" is that path — 69 of 125 findings
stay unfixable inside any single product, and each initiative keeps re-solving
identity, terminology and reporting.

**Watch for the follow-up:** "Why not just mandate a design system?" Because the
findings that matter span products — the audit says so — and a component library
doesn't settle who owns the journey across them.

---

## 5. "Isn't this a reorg?"

**Answer.** No. AE owns horizontal coherence — journey, IA, terminology, shared
interaction standards. Domain teams keep capability, economics and business rules.
Slide 25 draws that line and slide 26 assigns the decisions. Nothing moves org chart.

**Say it before it's asked** if the room skews to domain leaders.

---

## 6. "Your NPS is historical and mostly Sponsored Search. Why should I trust it?"

**Answer.** You shouldn't trust it alone, and the deck doesn't ask you to — it's
labelled directional, not a platform-wide baseline. The case rests on triangulation:
survey sentiment, a 125-finding platform audit, and observed behaviour in a different
product entirely (Display setup, 2,894 visitors across 2,214 accounts). Three methods,
three populations, same five problems.

**This is a strength — lead into it rather than defending.**

---

## 7. "0.89 correlation — are you claiming bad UX causes weak performance?"

**Answer.** No. It's a correlation across 424 paired responses. What it shows is that
advertisers don't hold the two apart: when the platform feels difficult they also stop
believing it works. That's a perception finding, not a causal one.

Slide 09 states this on the slide. Do not let it get restated as causation.

---

## 8. "Display — are you unwinding a committed programme?"

**Why it lands.** Slide 22 puts Goal-Based Buying and the template-first assumption
under "reassess." Whoever owns that programme will hear it as a cancellation.

**Answer.** Continue is the largest column on that slide. Reassess applies to two
assumptions, not the programme: whether templates alone simplify setup, and whether
the objective and bidding logic underneath them is sound. A cleaner setup flow can't
compensate for a weak decision model — that's the whole point of validating first.

---

## 9. "Agencies come out thinnest. Is that deliberate?"

**Answer.** It's surfaced, not decided. The appendix coverage slide shows agency
coverage as the thinnest in FY28 and explicitly says whether that's the right
trade-off is leadership's call. Asserting it as a settled choice would claim an
alignment nobody has made.

---

## 10. "When do we know it's working?"

**Answer.** The measurement framework is slide 32 — four levels, from task completion
through platform reuse. Review cadence already exists in governance: roadmap review
monthly, platform steering quarterly.

**Weak spot:** there are no targets, and only two baselines. If pushed on "what number
by when," say the targets follow instrumentation rather than inventing them.

---

## 11. "Why now?"

**Answer.** Commerce media grows ~70% to about $142B by 2030 (EMARKETER, Aug 2026),
and buyers are getting selective as it grows — 80% say programmatic access would make
it easier to shift budget, 49% expect a significant shift. The competitive bar is
moving from having capabilities to making them work together.

---

## Pre-empt in the deck vs hold for live

**Pre-empted already:** the causation guard (09), the historical-data caveat (09),
the counterfactual (03), the Display framing (22), agency coverage (appendix).

**Hold for live:** the reorg question, the "whose roadmap" question, and anything
about targets. These are better answered conversationally than by adding slides —
the deck is deliberately 31 slides and each of these would cost one.

**Bring on a card, not a slide:** the capacity number, once you have it.
