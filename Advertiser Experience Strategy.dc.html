Walmart Ads can turn experience into its next competitive advantage

Executive summary

The strategy in one page

Pressure

Competition is moving to ease and trust

Commerce media keeps growing, but capabilities are converging. Advertisers consolidate budget toward the platforms that cost them the least effort.

Problem

Our experience is fragmented, and we can measure it

One advertiser job crosses several platforms with different navigation, terminology and reporting. Advocacy has been negative in every measured month.

Choice

Change the order of delivery, not the ambition

Resolve information architecture, terminology and shared services first. Channel work then lands on those patterns instead of inventing its own.

Outcome

Each release leaves reusable foundations behind

Advertisers get a simpler platform now. Teams stop rebuilding the same front end. Intelligence arrives on foundations strong enough to trust it.

Platform audit findings

Advertisers should not have to translate Walmart to use Walmart

Ad Center presents channels, buying methods, statuses, service models and internal systems as equivalent choices. The experience should begin with the media advertisers intend to buy, then introduce mechanics and technology only when relevant. Language is part of the product.

All campaigns & campaign groups
Buying model

Use Display Auction for self-service campaigns. If auction and reserved inventory are both available, introduce the buying method during setup, where bidding, pricing and scheduling inputs differ.

Status vs. media

Keep campaign status separate from media type. Move Archived into the Status filter and apply one consistent status model across campaigns and reporting.

Media scope

Treat Social and other offsite media as distinct channels with different inventory, buyers and funding — not as extensions of Onsite Display. Connect them through shared navigation and reporting.

Abbreviations

Replace DSS, MS and other platform-specific abbreviations with clear customer-facing names that do not require knowledge of Walmart’s internal language.

Metadata-only

Prioritize customer-recognizable names over system-generated values. Surface IDs and platform codes only when they support search, troubleshooting or reporting.

Reporting
Channel definition

Reserve Channel for where media runs. Move DSPs, ad servers and other activation technology into separate platform metadata — The Trade Desk is a DSP; 1P DSP is an ad server, not a channel.

Product vs. platform

Do not combine an inventory environment with a buying platform. Organize reporting around Onsite Display and Offsite Media — “DSP” is not aligned with the industry definition, and Ad Center is also a DSP.

Ad solution’s role

Clarify Walmart’s role by media type: inventory owner for onsite placements and audience; activation and measurement partner across most offsite inventory.

Reporting context

Show total performance alongside channel-level contribution, and clearly identify differences in attribution, metric definitions and data freshness.

3%

of buyers overlapped between the two experiences, making the current grouping unintuitive for the overwhelming majority of users.

Terminology, taxonomy and product organization should reflect how advertisers understand media buying — not internal ownership.35
Platform audit findings

Shared products should not feel like they belong to separate platforms

Display and Sponsored appear to run on different Living Design foundations, causing the same platform to look, behave and communicate differently by channel. Advertisers must relearn core patterns as they move between hallways, while Walmart carries duplicated design and engineering effort.

Homepage purpose + priority

Sponsored leads with performance, recommendations and campaign creation; Display emphasizes reporting. Users can’t predict where to start or what matters first.

Navigation + orientation

Navigation structure, order and icon meaning change by product. Users must relearn the platform when they switch experiences.

Guidance + support

Sponsored provides more recommendations, next steps, training and feedback access; Display offers less explanation and assistance.

Filters + configuration

Filters, attribution controls and dashboard settings appear in different locations and use different interaction patterns. Familiar behavior does not transfer.

Metrics + reporting

Metric terminology, selection, visualization and disclaimer patterns are not normalized. Performance becomes harder to interpret and compare.

Voice + framing

Sponsored provides a guided, personalized entry point; Display uses a generic dashboard frame — the products feel separately designed.

Different media products may require different workflows. They should not require different platform fundamentals.

Strategic implications

Expand the ambition without expanding the near-term scope

What changes in the strategy
Design shared foundations so they can eventually support more than core Walmart Ads.
Avoid Walmart-specific decisions that would force another ground-up rebuild later.
Create a deliberate path to extend identity, navigation, workflows, components, APIs, reporting, creative, and intelligence.
Keep timing explicitly provisional until maturity, dependencies, and migration complexity are assessed.
Core message

Unify Walmart Ads first.

Build the shared core so Sam’s Club and priority international markets can plug into it later through configuration rather than reinvention.

Bigger ambition, unchanged near-term scope. The difference is what we refuse to hard-code.53
Business segment unification

Sam’s Club Connect may be the most practical first extension

Current hypothesis
The Sam’s Club Ad Center currently centers on Sponsored Ads.
Display has migrated to Artemis, but the offering is managed-service only.
The absence of mature self-service Display may reduce initial migration complexity.
Existing Walmart Ads foundations may therefore be reusable early.
Positioning

A likely first extension candidate — not a committed first migration. The strategy establishes the option; discovery determines the sequence.

Discovery questions
?Which Ad Center services, code, APIs, and components are already shared?
?How do identity, billing, reporting, measurement, and support differ?
?What is the future self-service strategy for Display after Artemis?
?Where is configuration enough, and where are distinct workflows required?
?What customer and advertiser overlap makes integration most valuable?
Structurally simpler than a full international rollout — but the scope still requires validation.54
Business segment unification

Extend Walmart International market by market

Avoid a single global migration
Markets may operate different platforms or service models.
Channel availability and self-service maturity differ.
Privacy, data residency, consent and regulation may constrain reuse.
Localization extends beyond language — currency, tax, billing, policy.
Some markets adopt the shared core; others need staged integration.
Required discovery
Current markets, platforms, products and roadmap ownership
Self-service versus managed-service maturity
Identity, account, billing, reporting and measurement models
Data residency, privacy and regulatory constraints
Localization and market-specific workflow requirements
Migration appetite, technical readiness and business value

Globally extensible. Locally configurable.

Standardize what should be common while preserving flexibility for market-specific products, policies, and advertiser needs.

Candidate waves, not a single global timeline.55
