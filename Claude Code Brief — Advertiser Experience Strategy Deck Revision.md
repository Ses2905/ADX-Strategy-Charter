# Advertiser Experience Strategy Deck
## Claude Code Revision Brief

### Objective

Revise the existing **One Walmart Ads Experience / Advertiser Experience Strategy & Team Charter** HTML presentation to incorporate the latest market research, quantitative evidence, behavioral data & Voice of Customer direction into the existing strategic narrative.

This is an **integration and refinement exercise, not a redesign of the strategy from scratch**.

The current strategic narrative has been intentionally refined over multiple iterations. Preserve its core thesis, four strategic priorities, team charter, execution model & decision-led structure.

The primary change is to restore sufficient evidence near the beginning of the presentation and connect that evidence explicitly to the strategic priorities throughout the deck.

The finished deck should feel:

- Executive-ready
- Evidence-led
- Strategically rigorous
- Visually sophisticated
- Concise without becoming superficial
- Clearly Walmart
- More visual than textual
- Consistent from slide to slide

Do not solve density by simply shrinking typography.

---

# Core Strategic Throughline

The presentation should make this argument:

**Walmart has differentiated advertising capabilities, but advertisers still have to do too much work to connect them. Market dynamics, customer research, behavioral data & VOC show that this is increasingly consequential. Advertiser Experience closes that gap by simplifying core work, making seller advertising more native, connecting shared platform capabilities & adding intelligence where it improves decisions without taking away appropriate advertiser control.**

A shorter articulation of the thesis is:

**Walmart's advertising capabilities create the opportunity. Advertiser Experience makes that opportunity easier to discover, activate, manage & grow.**

Every slide should advance this argument.

---

# Narrative Architecture

Use the following overall story:

**Opportunity → Decision → Market Pressure → Advertiser Evidence → Structural Diagnosis → Vision → Strategic Choices → First Applications → AE Charter → Execution Model → Roadmap → Measurement → Decision**

The evidence section should not feel like a detached research report.

Each type of evidence has a specific role:

**Market research**  
Establishes why the problem matters now.

**Quantitative customer research**  
Establishes the prevalence & persistence of advertiser friction.

**Behavioral/product data**  
Shows where advertisers actually struggle to complete work.

**Qualitative VOC**  
Explains what advertisers struggle with, what they are trying to accomplish & what they need instead.

**Journey/platform analysis**  
Connects those symptoms to structural product causes.

**Strategic priorities**  
Show what Walmart will change in response.

---

# Critical Narrative Rule

Do not structure VOC as:

**Here are all the things advertisers hate.**

Instead use:

**What advertisers struggle with → What they need to accomplish → What the strategy changes**

The presentation should demonstrate both pain AND desired progress.

Avoid creating a prosecutorial wall of negative customer quotes.

---

- Large unused areas caused by poor layout
- Content crammed into one portion of the slide
- Excessively narrow text columns
- Tiny footnotes

If a phrase can fit naturally on one line, do not force an arbitrary line break.

Cards in the same row should generally have equal visual weight & height.

---

# Visual Storytelling Requirements

Do not default to text boxes.

Actively convert concepts into:

- Journey maps
- Funnels
- Loops
- Architecture diagrams
- Before / after workflows
- Swimlanes
- Evidence matrices
- Connected systems
- Simple quantitative charts
- Decision flows
- Lifecycle diagrams

Use charts only when data supports them.

Do not invent data to make a chart.

---

# Slide Density

Aim for:

**One primary idea per slide.**

Use progressive hierarchy:

**Headline → visual argument → supporting evidence**

Not:

**Headline → six paragraphs**

When content becomes too dense:

1. Simplify the copy.
2. Convert relationships into visual structure.
3. Move supporting detail into notes or appendix.
4. Split a slide only when the additional slide materially improves the argument.

Do not shrink everything.

---

# Headline Style

Headlines should state the conclusion.

Good:

**Advertisers Need Better Planning, Control & Confidence in Results**

Bad:

**Advertiser Feedback**

Good:

**Shared Capabilities Should Travel With the Advertiser**

Bad:

**Unified Platform**

Good:

**One Advertiser Job Crosses Too Many Product Boundaries**

Bad:

**Current State Journey**

Every slide title should advance the narrative even if someone reads only the titles.

---

# Source Treatment

Every quantitative claim should have a source.

For internal data include, where relevant:

- Tool / research source
- Surface
- Date range
- Population
- Sample size
- Denominator

For external research include:

- Publisher
- Publication / report
- Year
- Forecast period where relevant

Use concise source footnotes.

Do not overwhelm slides with citation text.
